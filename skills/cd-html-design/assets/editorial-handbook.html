How to use

Read for decisions, not decoration

Each chapter moves from orientation to evidence. Ordinary prose remains flat; bordered structures appear only when the information type changes.

Working rule

Capture what happened, separate observation from interpretation, and record the next decision explicitly.

01Framing

Frame the question

Define the decision the research must support before choosing participants, settings, or evidence methods.

Start with one decision

A useful question names the actor, the situation, and the choice that follows. Keep the wording open enough to discover unexpected behavior.

STEP 01
State the decision
Write the choice the team must make after the study.
STEP 02
Set boundaries
Define who, where, and which situations belong in scope.
02Evidence

Gather evidence

Use a short sequence of preparation, observation, capture, and debrief so evidence remains comparable across sessions.

01 / PREPARE
Plan
Confirm context, consent, and the evidence format.
02 / OBSERVE
Watch
Record actions before assigning explanations.
03 / CAPTURE
Note
Preserve time, setting, signal, and uncertainty.
04 / DEBRIEF
Reflect
Separate fact, inference, and open question.
Stop condition

Pause the session when consent, safety, or confidentiality becomes unclear.

03Synthesis

Synthesize findings

Compare evidence across sessions, keep contrary signals visible, and connect each finding to the decision it informs.

Keep

Direct observations, recurring patterns, disconfirming cases, and confidence labels.

Avoid

Single-session certainty, unlabeled inference, decorative charts, and unsupported totals.

Field checklist
