# Life and boldness

A page can be perfectly aligned and still dull, or lively and not trusted. This file is about choosing how far to go and making the page respond like something alive. Nothing here is a requirement; take what fits the work.

## How bold to be depends on the reader

Boldness is a dial, not a goal. A client-facing report earns trust through restraint: a quiet palette, one data colour, real typographic hierarchy. A launch page or a personal project can carry far more colour and gesture. Before choosing, ask what the reader must feel to act on the content, and look for what the user has already approved.

Two failures sit at the ends of the dial. A page with no deliberate move reads as a template. A page with many reads as noise, or as younger and less reliable than its content. When unsure, show the options side by side instead of committing to one.

## Where a distinctive move can come from

One is usually enough. Anchor it to the grid so it reads as intent.

- **Scale.** Set the lead far outside the normal range: one figure or one sentence, with everything else quiet.
- **Asymmetry.** An unequal split, with a narrow column for labels or figures and a wide one for content.
- **The content as the graphic.** Make the data, the timeline, or the comparison the dominant visual instead of illustrating it.
- **Type as image.** A characterful display face used with restraint over a plain text face. Check that it carries the page's language well: a face that suits Latin can look strained in CJK.
- **Colour as structure.** One field of colour behind one section, or one colour reserved for the thing the finding points at.
- **A device from the subject.** Ledger rules for finance, a route line for logistics, margin notes for research.

## Interaction

- **Respond at once**, even when the result takes longer.
- **Let the reader touch the content** where it has dimensions: filter it, switch its view, follow a figure to the records behind it.
- **Keep state visible and shareable**: what is selected, how to clear it, and an address that restores it.
- **Give every control its states**: rest, hover, focus, active, disabled, and where relevant selected, loading, empty, and error.
- **Keyboard and pointer both work.** A chart with many marks is one stop in the tab order, with arrow keys inside it.
- **Do not gate content** behind a script or an animation. Enhance what is already there.

## Motion

Motion earns its place by showing a cause, a relationship, or a change of state. Short for small things, a little longer for large ones; animate transform and opacity so layout stays still; let a transition be interrupted; honour reduced motion. One orchestrated moment per page is usually plenty. Motion on every element reads as decoration.

## Before calling it done

- The lead is what a reader sees first, and the distinctive move is visible at arm's length.
- Someone who can see the page has looked at it, next to the reference if there is one.
- Every control was operated, in each state it has.
- With scripts off and with reduced motion on, the content is still there.
